If you work for yourself, you don't need to become an accountant. You need a simple system you'll actually keep up with: one bank account for the business, invoices for everything you earn, a receipt for everything you spend, and ten minutes a week. This page sets that system up, using only the parts of dubbl a one-person business needs.

<ShortVersion>

- Keep business money in its own bank account. Never mix it with personal spending.
- Invoice for every job, and attach a receipt to every business expense.
- Pay yourself by moving money to your personal account: an **owner's draw**, not a salary or an expense.
- Set money aside for tax as it comes in, so the bill is never a shock.
- Categorize weekly, reconcile monthly, close the year once.

</ShortVersion>

<Callout type="warn" title="Not tax advice">
Tax rules depend on where you live, how your business is set up and how much you earn. dubbl keeps the records and does the arithmetic; a tax professional can tell you what to file and what you can deduct.
</Callout>

**One business bank account** is the single most useful habit. When everything business goes through it and nothing personal does, your bank statement *is* your bookkeeping, and reconciling takes minutes.

## Paying yourself

As a sole owner, you usually don't pay yourself a salary through payroll. You simply move money from the business account to your personal one. In the books that's an **owner's draw**: it reduces your equity in the business, but it isn't an expense, so it doesn't lower your profit.

Putting your own money into the business works the other way round: debit the bank, credit **Owner's contribution**. In dubbl's US chart both are equity accounts (310 and 300); other countries' charts have their own equivalents.

## What to use in dubbl

You can ignore most of dubbl's menus. These are the ones that matter for a business of one:

- **[Invoices](/docs/guides/invoices)** for everything you earn. Use [recurring invoices](/docs/guides/recurring-invoices) for retainers.
- **[Bank accounts](/docs/guides/bank-accounts)**: import your statements and categorize each line. [Bank rules](/docs/guides/categorizing-transactions) handle the repeats (software subscriptions, rent) for you.
- **[Expenses](/docs/guides/expenses)** for things you paid personally that the business owes you back.
- **[Documents](/docs/guides/documents)** to keep receipts with the transactions they belong to.
- **[Reports](/docs/guides/reports)**: the profit and loss for how you're doing, and, in the US, [Schedule C](/docs/guides/schedule-c) to prepare your tax return.

Your Overview pulls it together: cash in the bank, profit so far, who owes you, and a to-do list of what needs you.

## Good to know

- Cash or accrual? As a solo business you can read your profit either way. [Accrual vs cash](/docs/learn/accrual-vs-cash) explains the difference.
- If you bought equipment (a computer, a camera), it may need to be [depreciated](/docs/learn/depreciation) rather than expensed at once. Ask your accountant.
- If you're registered for VAT, GST or sales tax, the tax you charge isn't yours. Keep it apart until the return is filed. See [sales tax and VAT](/docs/learn/sales-tax-and-vat).
- Ask [Tilly](/docs/guides/tilly), dubbl's assistant, "How did this month go?" for a plain-English summary.
