Accounting has a lot of words for simple ideas. Here they are in plain English. Where dubbl uses a friendlier label on screen, it's shown in *italics*.

## A

### Accounts payable (AP)
Money you owe suppliers for bills you haven't paid yet. A liability. See [receivables and payables](/docs/learn/receivables-and-payables).

### Accounts receivable (AR)
Money customers owe you for invoices they haven't paid yet. An asset.

### Accrual basis
Counting income when you earn it (invoice it) and costs when you incur them (are billed), whenever the money moves. *When invoiced or billed*. See [accrual vs cash](/docs/learn/accrual-vs-cash).

### Accrual
An entry that spreads a one-off amount over the months it belongs to, like a year of insurance paid up front. In dubbl, under **Accounting**, **Accruals**.

### Accumulated depreciation
The total value of an asset used up so far. *Value used up so far*. See [depreciation](/docs/learn/depreciation).

### Aged report
A list of unpaid invoices (aged receivables) or bills (aged payables), grouped by how far past due they are: Current, 1-30, 31-60, 61-90 and 90+ days.

### Asset
Something the business owns or is owed: cash, equipment, stock, unpaid invoices. Grows with a debit.

## B

### Balance sheet
A report of everything you own and owe on one date. *What you own and owe*. See [financial statements](/docs/learn/financial-statements).

### Base currency
The currency your books and reports are kept in. Foreign amounts are converted into it.

### Book value
What an asset is worth in your books: cost minus accumulated depreciation. *Current value*.

## C

### Cash basis
Counting income when money arrives and costs when money leaves. *When money moves*.

### Cash flow statement
A report of how your cash changed over a period, split into trading, investing and funding. *Where your cash went*.

### Chart of accounts
The list of every account in your books. See [the chart of accounts](/docs/learn/chart-of-accounts).

### Closing entry
The year-end entry that moves the year's revenue and expenses into retained earnings, starting them at zero for the new year.

### Cost of goods sold (COGS)
What the products you sold cost you to buy or make. An expense, recorded when the sale is.

### Credit
The right-hand side of a journal entry. Increases liabilities, equity and revenue; decreases assets and expenses. *Money out side* on the trial balance.

### Credit note
A document that reduces or refunds what a customer owes, the opposite of an invoice.

### Current year earnings
This year's profit so far, shown in the equity section of the balance sheet until the year is closed.

## D

### Debit
The left-hand side of a journal entry. Increases assets and expenses; decreases liabilities, equity and revenue. *Money in side* on the trial balance. See [debits and credits](/docs/learn/debits-and-credits).

### Depreciation
Spreading the cost of a long-lasting asset over its useful life. *Value drop*.

### Double-entry bookkeeping
Recording every transaction in at least two accounts, with equal debits and credits. See [double-entry bookkeeping](/docs/learn/double-entry).

### Draw (owner's draw)
Money an owner takes out of the business for themselves. Reduces equity; it isn't an expense.

## E

### Equity
What's left for the owners after everything owed is paid: money put in, plus profit kept. *What's left over (yours)*.

### Exchange gain or loss
The difference caused by an exchange rate moving between an invoice or bill and its payment. Realised when the money moves; unrealised while it's still open. See [multi-currency](/docs/learn/multi-currency).

### Expense
A cost of running the business: rent, wages, software. Grows with a debit and reduces profit.

## F

### FIFO (first in, first out)
A costing method that assumes the oldest stock sells first. See [inventory costing](/docs/learn/inventory-costing).

### Financial year (fiscal year)
The 12-month period your accounts run on. Doesn't have to match the calendar year.

### Fixed asset
Something the business owns and uses for more than a year, like equipment or a vehicle.

## G

### General ledger
Every journal entry line, grouped by account, with a running balance. *Every transaction, by account*.

### Gross pay
Pay before any tax or deductions. *Before deductions*.

### Gross profit
Sales minus cost of goods sold. *Profit after direct costs* on the executive summary.

## I

### Input tax (input VAT)
VAT or GST you paid suppliers, which you can usually reclaim. See [sales tax and VAT](/docs/learn/sales-tax-and-vat).

### Inventory
Stock held for sale. An asset until it sells.

## J

### Journal entry
The record of one transaction: a date, a description, and lines of debits and credits that add up to the same total.

## L

### Liability
Something the business owes: supplier bills, loans, tax collected. Grows with a credit.

### Lock date
The date up to which your books can't be changed. Set it under **Settings**, **Lock past dates**.

## N

### Net pay
What an employee takes home after tax and deductions. *Take-home*.

### Net profit (net income)
Revenue minus all expenses for a period. *Profit (money in minus money out)*.

## O

### Opening balances
The balances you start with in dubbl, carried over from your old system.

### Output tax (output VAT)
VAT, GST or sales tax you charged customers. Owed to the tax office.

## P

### Payment terms
How long a customer has to pay, like Net 30 (30 days after the invoice date).

### Prepayment
Paying, or being paid, in advance for something not yet delivered.

### Profit and loss (P&L, income statement)
A report of revenue and expenses over a period, and the profit or loss between them. *Money in vs money out*.

## R

### Reconciliation
Checking your books against a bank statement until they match. *Match statement to your books*. See [bank reconciliation](/docs/learn/bank-reconciliation).

### Residual value
What an asset is expected to be worth at the end of its useful life. Never depreciated. *Value left at the end*.

### Retained earnings
All the profit the business has kept over its life, in equity. Each closed year's profit is added to it.

### Revenue
Income from selling goods or services. Grows with a credit. *Money in*.

### Reversing entry
An entry that exactly undoes an earlier one, so both remain on record.

## T

### T-account
A sketch of an account as a big letter T: debits on the left, credits on the right.

### Trial balance
A list of every account's balance in debit and credit columns, which should add up to the same total. *Account balances check*.

## U

### Useful life
How long you expect to use an asset. Depreciation is spread over it.

## V

### VAT, GST and sales tax
Taxes on sales that a business collects for the government. VAT and GST let businesses reclaim tax on their own purchases; US sales tax doesn't.

### Void
Cancel a document or entry so it no longer counts. dubbl reverses its effect rather than deleting it.

## W

### Withholding
Tax taken from an employee's pay and sent to the tax office for them. See [payroll basics](/docs/learn/payroll-basics).

### Write-off
Recording an amount you'll never collect (or an asset that's lost its value) as an expense.
