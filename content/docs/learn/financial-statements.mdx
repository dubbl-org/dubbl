Three reports tell the whole story of a business. The **profit and loss** says whether you made money over a period. The **balance sheet** says what you own and owe on a given day. The **cash flow** explains why the money in the bank went up or down. Each one answers a different question, and the numbers on one feed straight into the others.

<ShortVersion>

- **Profit and loss** (in dubbl: *Money in vs money out*): income minus costs over a period.
- **Balance sheet** (*What you own and owe*): assets, liabilities and equity on one date.
- **Cash flow** (*Where your cash went*): how the bank balance changed, and why.
- Profit on the profit and loss becomes equity on the balance sheet.
- The cash flow starts from profit and ends at the change in cash on the balance sheet.

</ShortVersion>

## A period, or a moment

The first thing to know about any report is whether it covers a stretch of time or a single day.

<PhotoVsFilm />

The profit and loss and the cash flow are like a film: they show what happened between two dates. The balance sheet is like a photo: it shows where everything stood at the end of one day.

## The profit and loss

The profit and loss lists your revenue accounts (money in) and your expense accounts (money out) for a period, and subtracts one from the other. What's left is profit, or a loss if it's negative.

<Shot name="learn-pnl" alt="The profit and loss report: Money in, Money out and Profit cards, with a list of income accounts below" pins />

It's the report to read every month. Is money in growing? Is any cost growing faster than it should? It doesn't tell you how much cash you have: a month full of unpaid invoices can show a big profit and an empty bank. See [accrual vs cash](/docs/learn/accrual-vs-cash).

## The balance sheet

The balance sheet lists every asset, liability and equity account with its balance on one date, in three sections: **What you own**, **What you owe**, and **What's left over (yours)**. The first section always equals the other two added together: it's the [accounting equation](/docs/learn/double-entry) written out in full.

<Shot name="learn-balance-sheet" alt="The balance sheet: As of date, Compare to an earlier date, and the What you own section with account balances" pins />

Read it to answer: can the business pay what it owes? How much are customers holding back? How much of the business is really yours?

## The cash flow

Profit and cash are different things. You can be profitable and still run short of cash, if customers pay slowly or you buy a lot of equipment. The cash flow report reconciles the two. It starts from profit and adjusts for everything that didn't move cash, in three groups:

<Shot name="learn-cash-flow" alt="The cash flow report with From day-to-day trading, From buying & selling things, From funding and Change in cash" pins />

- **From day-to-day trading**: profit, adjusted for changes in what customers owe you, what you owe suppliers, and stock.
- **From buying & selling things**: equipment and other long-term things you bought or sold.
- **From funding**: loans taken or repaid, and money owners put in or took out.

The three add up to the **Change in cash** for the period.

## How the three connect

Here's a first year in business, all three reports side by side. The same numbers turn up in more than one place:

<StatementsTriad />

- **A**: the year's $35,000 profit appears on the profit and loss, as **Current year earnings** in the equity section of the balance sheet, and as the starting line of the cash flow.
- **B**: the cash flow ends at a $42,000 increase in cash. The business started with nothing, so that's exactly the bank balance on the balance sheet.

If you understand this picture, you understand how accounting fits together. Everything else is detail.

## Where to find them in dubbl

All three are in **Reports**, at the top under "The big picture": **Profit and loss**, **Balance sheet** and **Cash flow**. The profit and loss and the balance sheet can be downloaded as a PDF, and each can compare two periods or dates side by side.

## Good to know

- Profit for the current year shows on the balance sheet as **Current year earnings** until the year is closed. Then it moves into Retained Earnings. See [closing the books](/docs/learn/closing-the-books).
- Only finalized entries count. Draft journal entries and draft invoices don't appear in any report.
- The **Account balances check** report (trial balance) lists every account on one page. It's the quickest way to see that debits and credits agree.
