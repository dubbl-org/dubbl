The profit and loss report answers the simplest question about a business: did you make money? In dubbl it's called **Money in vs money out**. It adds up every income account and every cost account for the dates you pick, and the difference is your profit (or loss).

<Shot name="profit-and-loss" alt="Money in vs money out, with the date range, the basis switch, money in, money out and profit" pins />

## Read your profit and loss

<Steps>

<Step>

Open **Reports** and choose **Money in vs money out** under **The big picture**.

</Step>

<Step>

Pick the dates: **This Month**, **Last Month**, **This Quarter**, **Year to Date**, **This Year**, **Last Year** or **Custom**.

</Step>

<Step>

Under **Count income and costs**, leave **When invoiced or billed** on unless your accountant works on a cash basis.

</Step>

<Step>

Read the three cards at the top: **Money in**, **Money out** and **Profit (in minus out)**. A negative profit is a loss.

</Step>

<Step>

Scroll down for the detail. **Money in** lists each income account and **Money out** lists each cost account, with a total under each.

</Step>

<Step>

Choose **Download PDF** for a formatted copy or **Export CSV** for the account lines. Both use the dates and basis on screen, and amounts in the CSV are in dollars and cents (12.50, not 1250).

</Step>

</Steps>

## Good to know

- Profit isn't the same as cash in the bank. A sale you've invoiced counts now even if it's unpaid, and a loan you took out adds cash but no profit. See [Cash flow](/docs/guides/cash-flow) for the money side.
- Only posted entries count. Draft invoices and bills don't change the report until they're posted to your books.
- **Money out** includes the cost of the goods you sold as well as running costs like rent and wages.
- **When money moves** counts an invoice or bill when it's paid, not when it's issued. A part payment counts that share of the invoice, spread across its lines. Unpaid invoices and bills drop out, and entries that moved money themselves, such as categorized bank transactions, count on their own dates. See [Accrual vs cash](/docs/learn/accrual-vs-cash).
- To see the profit on one kind of work, pick a tag in the **No tag filter** box. The report then counts only records with that tag. See [Tags and cost centers](/docs/guides/tags-and-cost-centers#see-profit-for-one-tag).
- Tax you charge or pay isn't income or a cost. It sits in your tax accounts, so it never shows here. See [Tax rates](/docs/guides/tax-rates).

## From money in to profit

<ProfitWaterfall />

Every line on the report is one account from your [chart of accounts](/docs/guides/chart-of-accounts). If a cost lands in the wrong place, fix how the transaction was categorized and the report follows.

<Fold>

## Compare periods side by side

Choose **Compare two periods side by side** at the top of the report. Pick **Monthly**, **Quarterly** or **Yearly**, and how many periods to show (3 to 12). You get a chart of revenue, expenses and net income, and a table with the **Change** from each period to the next, in dollars and percent. The last period is always the current one.

<Shot name="profit-and-loss-comparison" alt="Four quarters of revenue, expenses and net income side by side, with the change between each" pins />

</Fold>

<Fold>

## Split by team or project

Choose **Split by team or project** to open **Profit by team or project**. It shows money in and money out for each department or project you tag transactions with, next to each other. Click an account to see the tagged transactions behind it. Tags are set up in [Tags and cost centers](/docs/guides/tags-and-cost-centers).

</Fold>

<Fold>

## A one-page yearly summary

**One-page yearly summary** gives you a short version for a whole calendar year: each income and cost account with its total, and the profit underneath. Pick the year at the top. For the current year it runs from January 1 to today.

</Fold>

<Fold>

## Why the numbers might not be what you expect

- **Profit looks too high:** check for bills you haven't entered yet, or bank spending that's still uncategorized.
- **Money in looks too low:** check for invoices still in draft, or dated outside the range.
- **A cost appears twice:** you may have entered a bill and also categorized its payment from the bank feed as an expense. Match the bank payment to the bill instead. See [Categorizing transactions](/docs/guides/categorizing-transactions). For bills or invoices entered twice, **Possible double-ups** in Reports lists likely copies.
- **Last year's numbers changed:** someone posted or edited an entry dated in that year. Lock finished periods to stop that. See [Periods and closing](/docs/guides/periods-and-closing).

</Fold>
