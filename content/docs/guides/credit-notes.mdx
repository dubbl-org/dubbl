A credit note is the opposite of an invoice: it takes money off what a customer owes you. Use one when you've overcharged, when goods come back, or when you agree to a discount after the fact. Once it's sent, the credit sits on the customer's account until you use it against one of their invoices.

<Shot name="credit-notes" alt="The credit notes list with credit left to use, totals and each note's remaining credit" pins />

## Issue a credit note

<Steps>

<Step>

Open **Sales › Credit notes** and choose **New Credit Note** in the top bar.

</Step>

<Step>

Pick the **Customer** and the **Issue Date**. Put the original invoice number in **Reference** so everyone knows what it's for.

</Step>

<Step>

Add a line for what you're crediting, with the same income account and tax rate as the original sale. Explain why in **Notes**.

</Step>

<Step>

Choose **Create Credit Note**. It's saved as a draft.

</Step>

<Step>

On the credit note, choose **Send**. **Send Email** emails it to the customer; **Mark as Sent** records it without an email. Either way it's now **Sent** and in your books.

</Step>

</Steps>

<Shot name="credit-notes-new" alt="The New Credit Note drawer for Acme Corp with reference INV-00004, one line and a reason in the notes" narrow />

## Good to know

- A credit note does nothing to your books while it's a draft. Sending it is what reduces your income and what the customer owes.
- Credit can only be used against invoices for the same customer, in the same currency.
- You can split one credit note across several invoices. Each use takes from what's left.
- No money moves when you use a credit. To hand money back, record the refund from your bank as well.
- **Cancel credit note** reverses it in your books and puts back any invoices it was used against. You can't undo it.

## Use the credit against an invoice

<CreditNoteOffset />

Open the sent credit note and choose **Use against an invoice**. Pick the **Invoice** from the customer's open invoices in the same currency, check the **Amount** (it can't be more than the credit left or what the invoice still owes) and choose **Reduce the invoice**. The invoice's balance drops; if the credit covers it all, the invoice is paid.

<Shot name="credit-notes-apply" alt="The Use this credit against an invoice dialog with an invoice picker, amount and remaining credit" narrow />

You can also start from the invoice. When a customer has credit on account, their open invoices show an **Available credit** box listing each credit, prepayment and overpayment, with a button to apply it.

## What gets recorded in your books

Sending a credit note undoes part of a sale. Your income goes down, the tax you'd have passed on goes down, and so does what the customer owes.

<JournalEntry
  title="Credit note CN-00003 sent to Acme Corp"
  date="Oct 13, 2026"
  lines={[
    { account: "Sales", debit: 30000 },
    { account: "Sales tax payable", debit: 3000 },
    { account: "Accounts receivable", credit: 33000 },

  caption="A $300 credit with 10% tax."
/>

Using the credit against an invoice doesn't post anything new. The credit and the invoice cancel each other out inside what the customer owes.

## Seeing what's left to use

The **Credit left to use** tile totals unused credit across all customers, and the **Left to use** column shows it per note. Each credit note's page shows its **Total**, how much has been **Applied** and what's **Remaining**. Customer [statements](/docs/guides/statements) list credit notes alongside invoices and payments.

</Fold>
