Every account your business keeps money in, or owes money on, gets a bank account in dubbl: current accounts, savings, credit cards, petty cash, a loan. Each one holds the lines from your bank statements, so you can say what every payment was for and check your books against the bank.

<Shot name="bank-accounts" alt="The Bank Accounts page with total balance, money in and out, and three accounts" pins />

## Add a bank account

<Steps>

<Step>

Open **Accounting**, then **Bank accounts**, and choose **New Account**.

</Step>

<Step>

Type the **Account Name** as you want to see it, the **Bank Name**, and pick the **Account Type**: **Checking**, **Savings**, **Credit Card**, **Cash**, **Loan**, **Investment** or **Other**.

</Step>

<Step>

Add the **Account Number / IBAN** if you like. Only the last digits are shown in lists.

</Step>

<Step>

Pick the **Currency** the bank holds the money in, and the **Country**.

</Step>

<Step>

Leave **Books connection** on **Set up automatically** unless you already have an account in your chart for it. Pick an **Accent Color** and choose **Create Account**.

</Step>

</Steps>

<Shot name="bank-accounts-new" alt="The New Bank Account drawer with name, bank, type, currency and books connection" narrow />

## Good to know

- dubbl doesn't connect to your bank directly. You bring transactions in by [importing a statement](/docs/guides/importing-transactions) from your bank.
- A new account starts with a zero balance in your books. If it had money in it before you started with dubbl, enter that in [Opening balances](/docs/guides/opening-balances).
- Every bank account is tied to an account in your [chart of accounts](/docs/guides/chart-of-accounts). That's where its money shows up on your balance sheet.
- An account can hold a currency other than your own. See [Multi-currency](/docs/guides/multi-currency).
- **Hide** masks every balance on the page, for when someone's looking over your shoulder.

## Inside an account

Choose an account to open it. The top shows its **Balance**, **Money In**, **Money Out**, and **Done**: how much of its statement lines are already in your books. Below that are four tabs:

- **Overview**: how many lines are **To do**, **Done** and **Ignored**, and the most recent ones.
- **Transactions**: every line, to [categorize](/docs/guides/categorizing-transactions) one by one or in bulk. **Apply rules** runs your bank rules on the lines still to do.
- **Imports**: each statement you've brought in. See [Importing transactions](/docs/guides/importing-transactions).
- **Settings**: the account's details.

**Import** brings in a new statement, and **Match statement to your books** starts a [reconciliation](/docs/guides/reconciliation).

## Moving money between your accounts

When you move money from one of your accounts to another, it isn't income or a cost. Choose **Move money**, pick **From** and **To**, enter the **Amount**, **Date** and an optional **Memo**, and choose **Record Transfer**.

<Shot name="bank-accounts-transfer" alt="The Move money between accounts drawer with From, To, Amount, Date and Memo" narrow />

dubbl records one entry that moves the money between the two accounts' places in your books, and adds a money-out line to one account and a money-in line to the other, linked together.

If the transfer already came in through a statement import, don't use **Move money**: open the line and choose **Record a transfer between accounts** instead, so it isn't recorded twice.

## Changing or deleting an account

On an account's **Settings** tab you can change its name, bank, type, number, currency, country and colour, set a **Low balance alert** amount so owners and admins get a [notification](/docs/guides/notifications) when the balance drops below it, and choose **Save changes**.

**Books connection** is the account in your chart where this bank account's money is recorded. Changing it only affects transactions recorded from then on; what's already in your books stays where it is.

**Delete account**, under **Danger zone**, permanently deletes the account and all its imported transactions.
