It's easy to end up with "Acme Corp" and "ACME Corp." as two contacts, especially after an import. Their invoices are split across both, so neither shows the real balance. dubbl spots likely duplicates for you, and merging moves everything onto the one you keep.

<Shot name="duplicate-contacts" alt="The Duplicates page explaining how contacts are grouped, showing that the contact list is clean" />

## Merge duplicates

<Steps>

<Step>

Open **Contacts › Duplicates**. Contacts that look like the same business are shown in groups.

</Step>

<Step>

In each group, pick the contact to keep with the round button on its row. It's marked **Keep this one**. dubbl suggests the one with the most records attached.

</Step>

<Step>

Choose **Merge into** followed by that contact's name, then **Merge them** to confirm.

</Step>

<Step>

If a group isn't really the same business, choose **Not duplicates** and it won't be suggested again.

</Step>

</Steps>

<DuplicateMerge />

## Good to know

- Contacts are grouped when they have the same name (ignoring capitals, punctuation and words like Inc, LLC or Ltd), the same email address, or the same tax number.
- Merging moves every invoice, bill, payment, quote, person and file onto the contact you keep, then removes the duplicate.
- A merge can't be undone. Check the group before you confirm.
- **Not duplicates** is remembered on this device only. Choose **Show dismissed** at the top, then **Suggest again** on a group to bring it back.
- Choose **Check again** after you've added or imported contacts.

<Fold>

## Merge from a contact's page

When you know the duplicate already, open the contact you want to keep and choose **Merge duplicates**. Search for the other contact, choose **Merge in**, then **Combine them**. Everything from the duplicate moves onto the contact you're on.
