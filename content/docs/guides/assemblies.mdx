If you make what you sell, a bill of materials is the recipe: which parts go into one finished item, how many of each, and what labour and overhead it takes. An assembly order builds a batch from that recipe. Completing it takes the parts out of stock, adds the finished items, and works out what each one cost.

## Set up a bill of materials

<Steps>

<Step>

Open **Inventory**, then **Bills of materials**, and choose **New bill of materials**.

</Step>

<Step>

Under **Makes**, pick the finished item. Give the recipe a **Name**, and enter the **Labour per unit** and **Overhead per unit** if building one costs you more than its parts. Choose **Create**.

</Step>

<Step>

dubbl opens the new bill of materials. Choose **Add part**, pick the **Part**, and enter the **Quantity per unit** (decimals are fine) and any **Wastage %**. Repeat for every part.

</Step>

</Steps>

<Shot name="assemblies-bom-new" alt="The New bill of materials drawer making a Monitor Stand, with a name and labour per unit" narrow />

The finished item and every part must already be [inventory items](/docs/guides/inventory). **Edit details** changes the name, labour, overhead or whether the recipe is **Active**; the pencil on a part changes its quantity or wastage.

## Build a batch

<Steps>

<Step>

On the bill of materials, choose **New assembly order**, or open **Assembly orders** and choose **New assembly order** there.

</Step>

<Step>

Enter the **Quantity to build**. **Parts this build uses** shows how many of each part you need against what's on hand. Choose **Create order**.

</Step>

<Step>

Choose **Start** when work begins, if you want to track it as **Building**.

</Step>

<Step>

When the batch is built, choose **Complete** and confirm. The parts leave stock, the finished items arrive, and the cost is recorded.

</Step>

</Steps>

## Good to know

- Completing an order checks that every part is in stock first. If one is short, nothing happens and you see which part and how many you need.
- Wastage is added to what's used and rounded up to whole units: 8 bolts with 10% wastage for 10 desks uses 88 bolts.
- Parts are taken out at their current cost (average or first in, first out) when you complete the order, so the finished item's cost reflects what you actually paid.
- **Start** only marks the order as **Building**. It doesn't set any stock aside.
- **Cancel** calls off a planned or building order. Nothing has been used yet, so stock doesn't change. A built order can't be cancelled.
- **Cost to build one** on a bill of materials is an estimate from the parts' current cost. The real cost is worked out when you build.
- An item can't be a part of itself, and each part appears once per recipe.

## What gets recorded in your books

Completing an order posts one entry. The finished items arrive in inventory at the total cost, the parts' value leaves inventory, and the labour and overhead are credited to a manufacturing clearing account.

The finished items go to the same Inventory account that sales and count changes take them out of, so the value always leaves the account it arrived in.

## With the API or Tilly

Everything here is also available through the [API](/docs/developers/api-reference) (amounts in cents) and [Tilly](/docs/guides/tilly):

```http
POST /api/v1/inventory/bom
{ "assemblyItemId": "<finished item id>", "name": "Standing Desk", "laborCostCents": 2000, "overheadCostCents": 500,
  "components": [{ "componentItemId": "<part id>", "quantity": 8, "wastagePercent": 10 }] }

POST /api/v1/inventory/assembly-orders
{ "bomId": "<bom id>", "quantity": 10 }

POST /api/v1/inventory/assembly-orders/<order id>/complete
```
