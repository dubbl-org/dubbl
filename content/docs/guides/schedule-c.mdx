If you're a US sole proprietor or single-member LLC, your business profit goes on Schedule C of your Form 1040. The **Schedule C Worksheet** adds up a year of your books and puts each total next to the Schedule C line it belongs on. It's a worksheet for you and your preparer, not a filing tool.

<Shot name="schedule-c" alt="The Schedule C Worksheet with the tax prep note, income against expenses and the line 31 net profit" pins />

## Get your Schedule C figures

<Steps>

<Step>

Open **Tax** in the sidebar and choose **Schedule C**. The tab shows for companies based in the US.

</Step>

<Step>

Check the period. It starts on the whole current year; choose **Last Year** when you're preparing last year's return.

</Step>

<Step>

Under **Count income and costs**, choose **When invoiced or billed** (accrual method) or **When money moves** (cash method, which most sole proprietors use). Pick the method you file with.

</Step>

<Step>

Read **Part I · Income** and **Part II · Expenses**. Each line lists the accounts behind it. The large figure is **Line 31 · Net Profit**, or **Net Loss** when costs beat income.

</Step>

<Step>

Choose **Export CSV** and send it to your preparer, or import it into your tax software.

</Step>

</Steps>

## Good to know

- There's nothing to set up. Each income and expense account goes on a line by its type and name, so the standard chart of accounts works straight away: **Marketing & Advertising** goes on line 8, **Rent Expense** on line 20b, **Wages & Salaries** on line 26, and so on.
- Costs of goods sold go on line 4. Interest, rental and other non-trading income go on line 6.
- Anything that doesn't match a line goes on line 27a, other expenses, such as bank fees and software.
- Income tax, unrealised currency gains and losses, and gains or losses on selling assets are left out. They don't belong on Schedule C.
- Line 3 is line 1 minus returns and allowances (line 2), and **Gross Income** is line 7: line 3, minus cost of goods sold, plus other income.
- Several accounts can land on the same line. Their totals are added together.
- The worksheet reads posted entries in your ledger, so invoices, bills, expenses, bank transactions and journals all count.

To force an account onto a particular line, open it from **Accounting**, **Chart of Accounts**, go to its settings and set **Sub Type** to a line code: `advertising`, `car_truck`, `commissions`, `contract_labor`, `depreciation`, `employee_benefits`, `insurance`, `interest_mortgage`, `interest_other`, `legal_professional`, `office_expense`, `pension`, `rent_lease_vehicles`, `rent_lease_other`, `repairs_maintenance`, `supplies`, `taxes_licenses`, `travel`, `meals`, `utilities`, `wages`, `other_expenses`, `income` or `returns_allowances`.

## What the worksheet doesn't do

- It doesn't file anything with the IRS, and it doesn't work out your self-employment tax or what you owe.
- It shows cost of goods sold as one total on line 4, not the Part III breakdown. Home office (line 30) and health insurance aren't filled in. Your preparer handles those.
- Meals show the full amount booked to the account. Usually only half is deductible; your preparer applies the limit.

</Fold>

## Getting the books ready for tax time

A worksheet is only as good as the books behind it. Before you export:

- Categorize every bank transaction for the year. See [Categorizing transactions](/docs/guides/categorizing-transactions).
- Reconcile each bank account to its statement. See [Reconciliation](/docs/guides/reconciliation).
- Read the [Profit and loss](/docs/guides/profit-and-loss) for the same year. Its profit should match line 31, apart from anything left out on purpose (income tax, unrealised currency movements, asset sales).
- If you paid contractors, check who needs a [1099](/docs/guides/form-1099).

</Fold>
