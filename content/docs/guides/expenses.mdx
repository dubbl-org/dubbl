An expense claim is for money someone paid out of their own pocket for the business: a train ticket, a client lunch, a software subscription on a personal card. They list what they spent, attach receipts, and submit it. Once it's approved, the business owes them that money until you pay it back.

<Shot name="expenses" alt="The expense claims list with totals awaiting approval and to reimburse, a chart by month and top people" pins />

## Submit an expense claim

<Steps>

<Step>

Open **Purchases**, then **Expenses**, and choose **New Expense**.

</Step>

<Step>

Give the claim a **Title**, such as "Client visit, Boston", and an optional **Description**.

</Step>

<Step>

For each thing you paid for, fill in the **Date**, **Description** and **Amount**, which is the full amount you paid, tax included. Add a **Category**, the **Account** it belongs to and the **Tax** rate shown on the receipt, and drop the receipt onto **Receipt**. Choose **Add Item** for the next one.

</Step>

<Step>

Choose **Create Expense Claim**. It's saved as a draft.

</Step>

<Step>

On the claim, choose **Submit**. It waits as **Awaiting approval** until someone approves or rejects it.

</Step>

</Steps>

<Shot name="expenses-new" alt="The New Expense Claim drawer with a title and one item for train tickets" narrow />

## Good to know

- For driving, tick **Mileage claim** on an item and enter the **Distance (miles)**. The amount is worked out from the **Rate per mile**, which starts at 0.67.
- Nothing reaches your books until the claim is approved. Approving records the cost and what you owe the person.
- An item with no account is recorded as Miscellaneous Expense, so pick the account if you can.
- When an item has a **Tax** rate, its amount includes the tax. The claim shows the tax on each item in the **Tax** column, and approving splits it out so you can reclaim it. Leave **Tax** on **No tax** if the receipt shows none.
- Rejecting needs a reason. The person can **Edit** the claim and **Resubmit** it.
- Approved by mistake? **Reverse** undoes the approval (and the payment, if there was one) and moves the claim back to a draft.

## Approving and paying back

On a claim that's **Awaiting approval**, choose **Approve**, or **Reject** with a reason. If an [approval workflow](/docs/guides/approvals) covers the claim, only the person whose turn it is can approve or reject, and the claim is approved (and booked) after the last step. Until someone decides, **Recall to edit** pulls it back to a draft so it can be changed.

When you've paid the person, open the approved claim, choose **Mark as Paid**, pick the **Payment Date** and choose **Confirm Payment**. To deal with many at once, select claims in the list and choose **Approve**, or **Mark paid today**.

</Fold>

## What gets recorded in your books

Approving a claim records each item's cost in its account, any tax you can reclaim in Input VAT / GST Receivable, and a debt to the person in Employee Reimbursements Payable. Here the train ticket (186.40) has a 20% tax rate and the lunch (62.00) has no tax:

<JournalEntry
  title="Expense claim approved: Client visit, Boston"
  date="Oct 14, 2026"
  lines={[
    { account: "Travel Expense", debit: 15533 },
    { account: "Meals & Entertainment", debit: 6200 },
    { account: "Input VAT / GST Receivable", debit: 3107 },
    { account: "Employee Reimbursements Payable", credit: 24840 },
  ]}
  caption="Each item's account gets its amount before tax. You still owe the person the full amount they paid. The entry is dated the day the claim is approved."
/>

Tax you can't reclaim, such as a blocked rate or US sales tax, stays in the item's account instead. A reverse charge rate records the tax on both sides, in Input VAT / GST Receivable and Output VAT / GST Payable, the same way it works on a [bill](/docs/guides/bills).

Marking it as paid clears that debt from your bank account:

## Expenses that repeat

For a cost someone claims every month, open **Recurring expenses** and choose **New Recurring Expense**. Give it a **Name**, the **Frequency** (weekly to annual), a **Start Date**, and optionally an **End Date** or **Max Occurrences**, then the lines. On each date dubbl creates a draft claim for you to check and submit.

</Fold>

<Fold>

## Paid with a company card instead?

If the business paid directly, from a company card or bank account, it isn't an expense claim: nobody needs paying back. Import the card or bank statement and [categorize the line](/docs/guides/categorizing-transactions) instead. For a supplier's invoice you'll pay later, record a [bill](/docs/guides/bills).

</Fold>
