Rent, software, insurance: some bills arrive with the same amount on the same day every period. A recurring bill is a template that creates that bill for you on a schedule, so you don't key it in again each month.

<Shot name="recurring-bills" alt="The Recurring Bills list with two monthly schedules, their suppliers and next bill dates" pins />

## Set up a recurring bill

<Steps>

<Step>

Open **Purchases**, then **Recurring bills**, and choose **New Recurring Bill**.

</Step>

<Step>

Give it a **Name** you'll recognize, like "Office internet", and pick the **Supplier**. Add a **Reference** if you want one on every bill.

</Step>

<Step>

Choose the **Frequency**: **Weekly**, **Fortnightly**, **Monthly**, **Quarterly**, **Semi-Annual** or **Annual**.

</Step>

<Step>

Set the **Start Date**, the date of the first bill. To stop it later, set an **End Date** or a number of **Max Occurrences**; leave both empty to keep going.

</Step>

<Step>

Add the **Line Items** (description, quantity, price, account and tax rate) and choose **Create Recurring Bill**. It shows up in the list straight away.

</Step>

</Steps>

<Shot name="recurring-bills-new" alt="The New Recurring Bill sheet with a name, monthly frequency and one line" narrow />

## Good to know

- Each bill is created as a **draft**. Check it against the supplier's real invoice, then choose **Add to my books**.
- Bills are created once a day. If dubbl missed some dates, the next run catches up and creates each missing bill.
- Each bill is dated on its scheduled date and is due after the supplier's payment terms, or 30 days if the supplier has none.
- When the last occurrence is created or the end date passes, the schedule shows **Finished**.

## Change, pause or stop a schedule

Each row in the list has three buttons on the right:

- **Edit** (or click the row) opens the schedule. You can change the name, supplier, reference, frequency, **Next Bill Date**, end date, max occurrences and every line. Choose **Save Changes**. Bills already created stay as they are; the changes apply from the next one.
- **Pause** stops new bills until you choose **Resume**. Dates that pass while it's paused are skipped, not caught up: when you resume, the next bill is the next scheduled date from today.
- **Delete** removes the schedule. Bills it already created stay.

## When the amount changes

A recurring bill always uses the amounts on its lines. If this month's invoice is different, open the draft it created and choose **Edit** to change the amount before you add it to your books. If the price has changed for good, **Edit** the schedule instead so every bill from now on uses the new amount. If the amount is never the same twice, a utility bill for example, skip the schedule and record each bill as it arrives.

</Fold>

## Recurring costs someone claims back

Repeating costs that an employee pays and claims back go under **Recurring expenses** instead, which creates draft [expense claims](/docs/guides/expenses) on a schedule.
