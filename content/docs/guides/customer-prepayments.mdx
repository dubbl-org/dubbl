Sometimes a customer pays before there's an invoice: a deposit to book your time, a payment up front for a big order, or more than they owed last time. Record it as a prepayment. The money goes into your bank straight away, but it isn't income yet. It waits as credit on the customer's account until you use it against an invoice.

<Shot name="customer-prepayments" alt="The prepayments page, empty in the demo company" />

## Record a prepayment

<Steps>

<Step>

Open **Sales › Prepayments** and choose **New prepayment** in the top bar.

</Step>

<Step>

Pick the **Customer**, the **Date** the money arrived and the **Amount**. Leave **Type** on **Prepayment**, or choose **Overpayment** if they paid more than an invoice asked for.

</Step>

<Step>

Under **Payment**, choose the account the money went into in **Paid into**. Add **Notes** about what it's for.

</Step>

<Step>

Choose **Record prepayment**. It's in your books now and shows as **Available**.

</Step>

</Steps>

<Shot name="customer-prepayments-new" alt="The New prepayment drawer for Pinnacle Group, 2,500.00, with notes about a deposit" narrow />

## Good to know

- A prepayment is recorded the moment you save it. There's no draft step.
- It isn't income until it's used against an invoice. Until then it's money you hold for the customer.
- You can use one prepayment across several invoices. Each use takes from what's left.
- Credit can only go against that customer's own invoices, in the same currency. Drafts and invoices waiting for approval aren't offered until they're sent.
- The customer's open invoices show their prepayments in the **Available credit** box, so you can apply it from either side.

## How a prepayment gets used

<PrepaymentFlow />

## Use it against an invoice

Open the prepayment and choose **Apply to an invoice**. The **Invoice** list only shows this customer's open invoices. Pick one, check the **Amount** and confirm. The invoice's balance goes down, and once the whole prepayment has been used it shows as **Used**.

<Fold>

## What gets recorded in your books

When the money arrives, your bank goes up and so does Customer Deposits, a liability: you owe the customer that money in goods or work.

When you use it against an invoice, the deposit is turned into payment for that invoice:

## The prepayments list

The tiles show what's **Available to use**, what's been **Used**, what you **Received this month**, the **Total received** and anything **Refunded**. The same list appears under **Contacts › Customer credits**.
