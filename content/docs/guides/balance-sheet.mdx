The balance sheet is a photo of your finances on one day. In dubbl it's called **What you own and owe**. It lists what the business owns (cash, money customers owe you, equipment), what it owes (bills, loans, tax), and what's left for the owners. The two sides always balance.

## Check your balance sheet

<Steps>

<Step>

Open **Reports** and choose **What you own and owe** under **The big picture**.

</Step>

<Step>

Pick the day next to **As of:**. It starts on today. For a year-end balance sheet, pick the last day of your financial year.

</Step>

<Step>

Read the three sections: **What you own**, **What you owe** and **What's left over (yours)**. Each ends with its total.

</Step>

<Step>

Click any account name to open its transactions in **Every transaction, by account**.

</Step>

<Step>

Choose **Download PDF** for a copy to send to your bank, investor or accountant.

</Step>

</Steps>

## Good to know

- It's a snapshot, so there's one date, not a range. Everything posted on or before that date counts.
- **What you own** minus **What you owe** always equals **What's left over (yours)**. If they didn't match, your books would be out of balance, and dubbl doesn't let that happen.
- **Current Year Earnings** under **What's left over (yours)** is the profit you've made that hasn't been closed into retained earnings yet. It's added for you so the sheet balances. See [Periods and closing](/docs/guides/periods-and-closing).
- Accounts with a zero balance still show, so you can see your whole chart.
- Amounts are in your company's base currency, the same as the rest of your books. See [Multi-currency](/docs/guides/multi-currency).

## The two sides always match

<BalanceSheetEquation />

Every transaction you record changes both sides by the same amount. An invoice adds to what customers owe you and to your profit; paying a bill takes from the bank and from what you owe. That's double-entry bookkeeping, explained in [Double-entry](/docs/learn/double-entry).

## Compare with an earlier date

Choose **Compare to an earlier date** and pick a second date. Each account gets another column, **As of** that date, so you can see what moved: how much cash you built up, whether customers owe you more than last quarter, how much of a loan you've paid down.

The button reads **Comparing to an earlier date** while it's on. Choose it again to go back to one date.

</Fold>

<Fold>

## Where each line comes from

| Section | What's in it | Where it comes from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **What you own** | Bank and cash, money customers owe you, stock, equipment, tax you can claim back | Your asset accounts |
| **What you owe** | Bills to pay, loans, tax you've collected, wages owed | Your liability accounts |
| **What's left over (yours)** | Money you put in, profits kept from earlier years, **Current Year Earnings** | Your equity accounts, plus profit not yet closed |

Click an account and the ledger opens at that account, from the first day of the financial year that contains your **As of:** date, up to that date. Your financial year comes from **Accounting** > **Financial years**, or **Fiscal year start** in your organization settings. The ledger starts with the account's opening balance on that first day, so the last running balance matches the amount on the balance sheet. See [General ledger](/docs/guides/general-ledger).

</Fold>

<Fold>

## Things to check before you send it

- Bank accounts here should match your bank statements on the same date. If not, [reconcile](/docs/guides/reconciliation) first.
- **Accounts receivable** should match the total in **Who owes you (and how late)**, and **Accounts payable** the total in **What you owe (and when it's due)**, for the same date. See [Aged receivables and payables](/docs/guides/aged-receivables-and-payables).
- Odd balances in suspense or clearing accounts usually mean something is still waiting to be categorized or matched.

</Fold>
