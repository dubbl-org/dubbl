Profit and cash in the bank are rarely the same number. The cash flow report, **Where your cash went**, follows the actual money: how much came in and went out over a period, split into day-to-day trading, buying and selling things, and funding. The bottom line is how much your cash changed.

<Shot name="cash-flow" alt="Where your cash went, with the three sections and the change in cash" pins />

## See where your cash went

<Steps>

<Step>

Open **Reports** and choose **Where your cash went** under **The big picture**.

</Step>

<Step>

Pick the dates: **This Month**, **Last Month**, **This Quarter**, **Year to Date**, **This Year**, **Last Year** or **Custom**.

</Step>

<Step>

Read the cards at the top: one total for each section and **Change in cash** for the whole period.

</Step>

<Step>

Scroll down for the lines in each section, then **Total change in cash this period** at the bottom.

</Step>

</Steps>

## Good to know

- The report starts from your profit and works back to cash, the way accountants usually present it. That's why the first line under **From day-to-day trading** is your net income.
- Money customers still owe you, and bills you haven't paid yet, appear as adjustments. More owed to you means less cash than profit; more bills unpaid means more cash than profit.
- Changes in other short-term items, such as tax you've collected but not paid yet, wages owed or money paid in advance, show as **Change in other current items**.
- Depreciation is added back, because it lowers profit without any money leaving.
- **From buying & selling things** covers equipment, property and other long-term items. **From funding** covers loans, owner money in and repayments. dubbl sorts your accounts into these sections by what they're used for in your chart of accounts, so it works whatever you've named them.
- There's no basis switch here. Cash flow is always about money that actually moved.
- This report has no PDF or CSV button, but it's included in **Download a report bundle**. See [Reports](/docs/guides/reports#exporting-and-sharing).

## Cash coming up

**Cash coming up**, under **Planning ahead** in Reports, looks forward instead of back. It lists money you expect in from unpaid invoices and out on unpaid bills, by due date, plus repeating invoices and bills you've set up. Choose how far ahead to look: **4 weeks** to **52 weeks**.

<Shot name="cash-flow-forecast" alt="Cash coming up, with money expected in and out and a week-by-week breakdown" />

You get **Money expected in**, **Money expected out** and **Expected change in cash**, a chart of the running total, and a **Weekly Breakdown** table. Invoices already past their due date aren't included, so chase those separately with [Aged receivables](/docs/guides/aged-receivables-and-payables).

</Fold>

<Fold>

## Bank cash flow

**Bank cash flow**, under **Save, schedule & reconcile**, is the simplest view of all: deposits in and payments out of your bank and cash accounts, grouped into periods, with a running balance and no accounting adjustments. Use it when you only want to know what went through the bank.

</Fold>
