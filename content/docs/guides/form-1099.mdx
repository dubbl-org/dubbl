In the US, when you pay a contractor $600 or more in a year, you send them (and the IRS) a 1099. dubbl keeps a running total for every supplier you mark as a 1099 contractor and, at year end, tells you who crossed the line.

<Shot name="form-1099" alt="The 1099 vendor totals report for this year: two contractors, both paid over the $600 threshold and needing a form" pins />

## Track a contractor for a 1099

<Steps>

<Step>

Open the contractor in **Contacts**.

</Step>

<Step>

Under **Payment**, find **US tax reporting** and tick **Track this supplier for a 1099**.

</Step>

<Step>

Fill in the details from their W-9 that appear underneath: **W-9 tax classification**, **Tax ID (SSN or EIN from their W-9)**, and tick **Backup withholding** if you have to hold back 24% of their payments.

</Step>

<Step>

Choose **Save changes**.

</Step>

<Step>

At year end, open **Reports** and choose **1099 vendor totals (US)** under **Tax time**.

</Step>

<Step>

Pick the year next to **Tax year:**. It starts on last year, which is usually the one you're filing for.

</Step>

<Step>

Read the table. **Needs a form?** says yes for everyone paid at or above the threshold. Choose **Export CSV** to take the list to your 1099 filing service.

</Step>

</Steps>

## Good to know

- Only payments you record as paid out to the contractor count, such as paying their bills, dated inside the calendar year. Spending you categorize straight from the bank feed isn't a payment record, so it isn't added.
- Card payments are left out, because the card company reports those itself.
- The threshold is $600 for the whole year. **Reporting threshold** at the top shows the one in use.
- A contractor you track but didn't pay still shows, with $0, so you can see your whole list.
- **Tax ID** uses the W-9 tax ID on the contact, or the contact's **Tax number** when that's empty. It shows **missing** when neither is filled in.
- **Export CSV** writes the totals as plain amounts (1250.00).
- dubbl doesn't print or e-file the 1099 forms themselves. Use the exported list with your filing service.

## Who needs a form

<Form1099Threshold />

The summary cards add it up for you: **Need a 1099 form** counts the contractors at or above the threshold, and **Total paid to those contractors** is what you'll report across all of them. Anyone flagged for backup withholding gets a warning next to their name.

<Fold>

## Getting ready before January

- Mark contractors when you first add them, not at year end. Totals are worked out from payments, so marking someone late still counts the whole year.
- Collect a W-9 from each contractor before you pay them, and save the details on their contact.
- Pay contractors through their bills or as payments in dubbl, not only from the bank feed, so the payments are counted.
- Check the list again after the year's last payments clear.

</Fold>
