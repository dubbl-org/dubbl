A requisition is an internal request to spend money: "we need three laptops for the new hires by the 1st". It isn't sent to a supplier and never touches your books. It gives whoever holds the budget a chance to say yes or no before anyone places an order.

<Shot name="requisitions" alt="The requisitions list with what is waiting for approval, approved and ordered, top suppliers, and three requests with their status" pins />

## Ask to buy something

<Steps>

<Step>

Open **Purchases**, then **Requisitions**, and choose **New requisition**.

</Step>

<Step>

Pick the **Supplier** if you know who to buy from. You can leave it empty and choose it later, but it's needed before the request becomes a purchase order.

</Step>

<Step>

Check the **Request Date** and set **Required By**, the date you need it. Add a **Reference** and **Notes** to explain why.

</Step>

<Step>

Add a line for each thing you need: a description, **Qty**, an estimated **Unit Price** and, if you like, the **Account** it should be booked to. Choose **Add Line** for more.

</Step>

<Step>

Choose **Create Requisition**. It's saved as a draft.

</Step>

<Step>

Open it and choose **Submit**. It waits as **Waiting for approval**.

</Step>

</Steps>

<Shot name="requisitions-new" alt="The New Purchase Requisition sheet with a supplier, dates and one line for laptops" narrow />

## Change a request

While a requisition is a draft, open it and choose **Edit**. You can change the supplier, the dates, the reference, the notes and every line. Choose **Save Changes** and the total is worked out again. Once it's submitted it can't be changed, unless a manager rejects it.

## Good to know

- A requisition is only a request. Nothing is ordered and nothing is recorded in your books.
- The unit prices are estimates. The real prices go on the purchase order and the bill.
- A requisition needs a supplier before it can become a purchase order. If it doesn't have one yet, dubbl asks for it when you order.
- **Required By** becomes the expected delivery date on the purchase order.

## Approving or rejecting

Open a requisition that's **Waiting for approval**. Choose **Approve** to say yes, or **Reject** and give a reason so the person knows what to change. The list's **Waiting for approval** and **Needed this week** totals show what needs a decision first.

A rejected requisition shows the reason at the top. The person who asked can choose **Edit** to change it, then **Resubmit** to send it for approval again. The old reason is cleared.

</Fold>

<Fold>

## Turning it into a purchase order

On an approved requisition, choose **Create a purchase order**. If the requisition has no supplier yet, dubbl asks **Who are you ordering from?** first: pick the **Supplier** and choose **Create a purchase order** again. dubbl makes a draft [purchase order](/docs/guides/purchase-orders) for the supplier, dated today, with the same lines, quantities, accounts and totals, and the delivery date set to **Required By**. The requisition becomes **Ordered**, with a **View PO** link. Check the prices with the supplier, then send the order as usual.
