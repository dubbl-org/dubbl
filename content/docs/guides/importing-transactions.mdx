dubbl doesn't log in to your bank. Instead, you download a statement from your online banking and import it. Each line on the statement becomes a transaction on the bank account, waiting for you to say what it was. Most banks offer at least one of the formats dubbl reads, and CSV works everywhere.

<Shot name="importing-transactions-sheet" alt="The Import Statement sheet with pasted CSV lines and a preview showing format, rows, duplicates and the lines" pins narrow />

## Import a statement

<Steps>

<Step>

Download a statement from your bank's website, for the dates you need.

</Step>

<Step>

In dubbl, open **Accounting**, then **Bank accounts**, pick the account, and choose **Import**.

</Step>

<Step>

Drop the file onto **Drop your statement here**, or click to browse. If your bank only shows the lines on screen, copy them into **Or Paste Content** instead.

</Step>

<Step>

Leave **Format** on **Auto Detect**, or pick it if you know it. Choose **Preview** to check what dubbl found before anything is saved.

</Step>

<Step>

If the rows, dates and amounts look right, choose **Import**. The new lines show up on the account as **To do**, and the message tells you how many your bank rules dealt with.

</Step>

</Steps>

## Good to know

- Lines you've already imported are skipped, so overlapping statements are fine. The preview tells you how many **Duplicates** it found.
- Money out should be negative and money in positive. If your bank's CSV has separate **debit** and **credit** columns, dubbl reads those instead.
- After an import, the account's balance is set to the statement's closing balance, or the running balance on its last line.
- Importing on its own doesn't change your books. A line only counts once it's [categorized](/docs/guides/categorizing-transactions).
- [Bank rules](/docs/guides/categorizing-transactions#bank-rules-let-dubbl-remember) run on every import. Lines a rule recognises get a suggested category, or are categorized straight away if the rule has **Categorize matching transactions automatically** turned on.
- Made a rule after importing? **Apply rules** on the **Transactions** tab runs your rules on the lines still to do.

## Formats dubbl reads

| Format | Where it comes from |
| --- | --- |
| **CSV**, **TSV** | Almost every bank. A spreadsheet of lines. |
| **OFX**, **QFX**, **QBO** | "Download for Money / Quicken / QuickBooks" options. |
| **QIF** | Older Quicken exports. |
| **CAMT.052**, **CAMT.053**, **CAMT.054** | European banks (ISO 20022 XML). |
| **MT940**, **MT942** | SWIFT statements from business banks. |
| **BAI2** | US business and treasury banking. |

**Auto Detect** works out the format from the file's contents, so you rarely need to pick one.

</Fold>

## Getting a CSV to import cleanly

dubbl looks for a header row and recognises common column names:

- **Date**: date, transaction date, posting date, posted date, book date.
- **Description**: description, memo, details, narrative, particulars.
- **Amount**: amount, value or sum. Or separate debit / withdrawal and credit / deposit columns.
- **Balance** (optional): balance or running balance.
- **Reference** (optional): reference, ref, transaction ID, FITID or check number.

If a preview shows zero rows or the wrong amounts, rename the columns in your spreadsheet to match these, save it as CSV, and try again.

</Fold>

## Your import history

The account's **Imports** tab lists every statement you've brought in: the file, its format, when you imported it, how many rows it had and how many duplicates were skipped. **Import Statement** there opens the same import sheet.
