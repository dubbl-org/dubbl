When a customer pays up front for a year of support or a six-month project, you haven't earned it all on day one. A revenue schedule takes the invoice's income out of this month and puts it back a month at a time, so your profit and loss shows the money in the months the work was done.

<Shot name="revenue-schedules" alt="The revenue schedules list with what's left to count, periods ready to count and each schedule's progress" pins />

## Set up a schedule

<Steps>

<Step>

Open **Sales › Revenue schedules** and choose **New revenue schedule** in the top bar.

</Step>

<Step>

Pick the **Invoice** the income comes from. Only sent invoices are listed, shown by number and customer. If the invoice's lines use different income accounts, pick the **Line** to spread; otherwise you can spread the **Whole invoice**.

</Step>

<Step>

Check the **Amount to spread**. It starts at the invoice's (or line's) amount before tax; enter less to spread only part of it. Choose the **Method**, then set the **Start Date** and **End Date**. dubbl makes one period for each month from the start month to the end month.

</Step>

<Step>

Choose **Create revenue schedule**. The amount moves out of income into deferred revenue straight away, and is split evenly across the periods; any leftover cent goes on the last one.

</Step>

</Steps>

<Shot name="revenue-schedules-new" alt="The New revenue schedule drawer with an invoice picker, the amount to spread, the method and start and end dates" narrow />

## Count each month's income

With **Even amounts over time**, dubbl counts each month's share as income overnight once the month has started, so there's nothing to do. A period that has started and isn't counted yet shows **Ready to count**; to count it straight away, open the schedule and choose **Count next period**. That month's share moves out of deferred revenue and into your income. When every period is counted the schedule is **Finished**.

<Shot name="revenue-schedules-preview" alt="A twelve-month schedule for a Riverdale Co invoice, with the past months counted and this month ready to count" narrow />

## Good to know

- **Even amounts over time** is counted automatically each month.
- **By milestone** is split by month too, but nothing is counted for you. Choose **Count next milestone** when each part of the work is done.
- **All on completion** keeps the whole amount in deferred revenue and counts it in one go on the end date.
- Periods are counted one at a time, oldest first. A period is never dated before the invoice or in the future.
- **Cancel schedule** stops further counting and puts the income that wasn't counted yet back into your income today. Periods already counted stay as they are.
- An invoice with a schedule in progress can't be cancelled. Cancel the schedule first. Choose **View invoice** on a schedule to open its invoice.

## What gets recorded in your books

Creating a schedule posts one entry on the invoice date that moves the amount out of income and into deferred revenue (money you've been paid but haven't earned yet, a liability). On the standard chart that's **2400 Unearned Revenue**; on a Xero-style chart it's **Income in Advance**. If your chart has no such account, dubbl adds one the first time it's needed. Each period counted then posts one entry: deferred revenue goes down, and income goes up. The income account is the one on the invoice line you spread.

Cancelling a schedule posts the same kind of entry for what was left: deferred revenue goes down and income goes up by the amount not counted yet.
